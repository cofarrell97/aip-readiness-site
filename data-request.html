Bringing your own data to the next session

To make the next session about your organization rather than a generic demonstration, we would like to work from a small sample of what you already use to plan investment. Four things help most. Send whatever exists today; partial is fine.

As it is todayNothing needs to be created, cleaned or reformatted.
A sample, not everythingOne or two asset classes and a handful of cases are plenty.
Gaps are expectedBlanks and inconsistent values tell us something useful too.
What to send
01

Business cases

3 to 5 recent business cases, as they exist today, including the template if there is one.

A useful mix: one approved, one declined or deferred, one asset replacement driven by risk, and one driven by growth or opportunity.

Different formats and levels of detail are fine. Where the case compared alternatives, include them.

02

Investment list or capital plan

The full list from the last planning cycle, including items that were not funded.

Columns that help
Project ID and nameBusiness unitAsset class Driver (risk, growth, compliance)Total cost and cost by year Start and end datesStatus (approved, deferred, rejected) Priority or score, if used

Unfunded items show how trade-offs were made. A must-do flag helps if one exists.

03

Asset sample

One or two asset classes, ideally those with the most capital spend. A few hundred to a few thousand rows is plenty.

Minimum
Asset IDTypeInstall or in-service date Replacement costCondition
Helpful if available
Location or regionParent siteProbability of failure Consequence or criticalityFailure and maintenance historyLinked project ID

Blanks and inconsistent values are expected. The whole estate is not needed.

04

Risk and value documents

The risk matrix, with probability and consequence definitions and consequence categories (safety, environment, customer, financial, reputation).

Corporate objectives, and any scorecard, prioritization criteria or weights.

A link or an existing document is fine.

How to send it

Send what you have to your IFS Copperleaf contact. If the files are large or sensitive, ask them for a secure way to share.

If something is hard to pull together, or can't be shared, just tell us. A conversation about how it works today is useful too.
